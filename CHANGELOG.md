# Changelog

All notable changes to arc (Android and web). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/):
a major version breaks `.pak` or library compatibility, a minor version adds features, a patch
version fixes bugs. The version itself is set in `version.properties`.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Live on Android: **Make up for Bluetooth delay**, a switch in Settings → Live (on by default; its info key says what it does and the delay now). While the sound goes to Bluetooth or a hearing aid, Live reads the output's own latency about once a second (Oboe's estimate on the native engine, the frames in flight by the output's timestamp on AudioTrack) and makes up the part of a Bluetooth delay that the output's clock leaves out: a wireless output is taken to delay 180 ms at least, less what the measured latency already counts, and all of it when nothing can be measured. The pattern's clock is arc's own, so its notes are not sent earlier; the playhead, the count-in and TEMPO's light follow what is heard, and a pad or key recorded into a pattern is placed the delay before it was made (a press just before the loop comes round lands on the beat, one early in a pass at the end of the pass before; counted in, a downbeat a little early is the downbeat, and in a run a pad started, a chord's other fingers land on it with the first). The click that follows the EP-133's MIDI clock is sent earlier by that delay and heard on the device's beat. The Bluetooth key's toast says the delay is made up for (**Bluetooth plays late (about 180 ms): arc makes up for it**). Wired and the speaker are unchanged, bit for bit. Nobody has played it over Bluetooth yet.
- Live on Android: while the sound goes to Bluetooth or a hearing aid, Live's top bar keeps a small amber **Bluetooth key** before the connection key, for as long as it does: square and the connection key's size, a Bluetooth glyph and a clock drawn like the other icons in dark ink on a new `warn` amber, tagged in the **?** guide. A tap shows **Bluetooth plays late: wired or the speaker is quicker** as a toast (over the bar's middle on a phone on its side, as other toasts); a long press names it (**Bluetooth delay**) and a screen reader reads the sentence. The display line stays as it was, with no chip: it says "Press a pad" or the hit. (Before, the sentence took the line's place, where a phone cut it to "Bluetoot…" and the first pad hit hid it.)
- Live on Android: **patterns, scenes and copy/paste**, the EP-133's GROUP and MAIN with − / + or a number, COMMIT, ERASE + MAIN held, the scene change setting, and SHIFT + C / D, on the phone. Each group now holds up to 99 patterns of its own length and a project up to 99 scenes (a scene is the pattern each group plays); the one pattern per group before this is pattern 1 of scene 1. An **S01** chip after ▶ on the stopped line (and, where there is room, while it plays) unrolls the line into the **SCENE panel** over the function keys, where STEP's goes; while the pattern plays and there is more than one scene, or a group is on a pattern other than 1, the line reads **S02 · A01 B03→05 C01 D02** instead, the number waiting blinking (steady with animations off). The group keys carry the patterns' numbers in the same cases. On the panel's dark screen: ▶ (■), the scene's − and + (past the last, a new scene on each group's next free pattern), four group columns with the pattern's number (a tap opens the **1–99 grid** over the pads: patterns with notes filled, the one playing orange, the next free one outlined), − and + and **NEXT FREE**; **COMMIT** (the scene copied after itself, into free slots); **CLR**, held 2 s (**DEL** on an empty scene): the scene's notes cleared or the scene deleted; **CHANGE**, cycling Immediate, Bar end and Pattern end, also in the Pattern sheet as **SCENE CHANGE**; and **CLIP**: **PTN**, **BAR** (with the focused group's bar pages) or **PAD**, **COPY** and **PASTE** (PAD: COPY, a tap on a pad, PASTE, a tap on the target, in any group). Unlike STEP's the panel stays open while the pattern plays, the pads stay playable under it (but for the PAD clip's two taps), and a pick made while playing takes over at the next bar line or at the end of the pattern, sample-accurately, the old pattern's notes ringing out (Immediate: on the spot; stopped, always at once), a group's pick replacing its own and a scene's cancelling the groups'. STOP applies what waits and UNDO drops it. COMMIT, CLR / DEL and every paste are one UNDO step, which restores the scenes and patterns shown. Patterns are saved in `patterns.json` version 2 (version 1 still reads). A pattern switched in starts at its own bar 1 on the tick it takes over, as the device starts it (a 2-bar pattern switched in on an odd bar plays its bar 1 first, and what is recorded into it, the playhead, ERASE and CORRECT held and AUTO length all count from there; Bar end and Pattern end are the pattern's own lines from where it started, a scene's Bar end the transport's); PLAY starts every pattern at bar 1 again. The clipboard stays when the project changes (until arc is closed), so a pattern, bar or pad's notes copied in one project pastes in another, and nothing goes to the EP-133. Screen readers hear the scene, each change once, the keys, the hold key's long click and every grid cell. The web has the core (the model, copy and paste, undo, the file, the text and where each pattern started) with the same tests, but no screens yet. Not yet tried on a phone.
- Live on Android: **step sequencing and timing correct**, the EP-133's − / + while stopped, RECORD + pad, SHIFT + KNOB X / Y, SHIFT + pad then − / +, and SHIFT + TIMING. A **STEP** chip on the stopped display line (its strip glyph where the line is short) unrolls the line into the **STEP panel** over the function keys, as SAMPLE's panel unrolls, on one group's pads and KEYS' grid upright and one group's pads on its side (not with all four groups, on the piano or on KEYS' grid on its side). On its dark screen: the panel's held **RECORD**, ▶ and a status (**STEP 1.2.1**, **+ SNARE**, **KICK → 1.2.2**, **KICK +1 TK**, **5 CORRECTED**); − and + either side of a strip of one bar of steps at TIMING's interval (dots for notes, the cursor orange, a tap jumps, **BAR** pages a longer pattern), the notes on the step sounding as the cursor lands; **VEL** and **LEN** knobs for every note on the step (LEN from a tick to a bar; one UNDO step per turn); **NUDGE** and **CORRECT**. Hold RECORD and tap pads or KEYS keys to put them on the step, one interval long, at the touch's pressure or full velocity, the same pad and pitch replaced; without it a press only sounds, and in the panel nothing arms or goes to the arp. The pads on the cursor's step are outlined. A long press (or NUDGE and a tap) picks a pad with a note there, ringed and tagged NUDGE, for − / + to move its note a step on the swung grid (a tick in free time), the cursor following. CORRECT puts a tapped pad's notes on TIMING's grid and swing, and with a pad held − / + move all its notes a tick; while the pattern plays it stays lit on the line, on every page, and a pad held corrects its notes as they pass, counted on the line, one UNDO step a hold (two pads held together, a tap on one with another held too, are one step, from the first down to the last up). CORRECT and ERASE turn each other off, as STEP and SAMPLE (and EDIT) do. The cursors are kept per project and group while arc runs. Screen readers hear the cursor, each change once, the strip's steps, the knobs and the latches as switches, and the pads have a **Pick to nudge** action. Nothing goes to the EP-133; the web has the core (the steps, the edits and the text) with the same tests, but no screens yet.
- Live on Android: **the arp and note repeat**, the EP-133's TIMING + pads, on a switch. **ARP / RPT** sits on the pads' plate under KEYS / PADS (one group and KEYS' grid, upright, on its side and on a tablet; over the piano in its row; in Live tools with all four groups): in KEYS (**ARP**) the keys held arpeggiate, in PADS (**RPT**) every pad held repeats on each step, at TIMING's interval. It lights orange while on, and **LATCH** under it keeps the notes going after the fingers lift, until a press after every finger is up starts a new set. The notes held are outlined (KEYS numbers them in the order pressed), the one sounding is lit, and the display line says what plays: **ARP · 1/16 · DO FA LA**, **ARP ∞ · …** latched, **REPEAT · 1/16 · A 7**. Where the screen reports pressure, a held key's or pad's pressure is its velocity. With RECORD on, the arp's notes go into the pattern like played ones. Each switch ticks under the finger with Haptics on, reads as a switch with its state (LATCH, while ARP is off, says to turn it on first) and has its tag in the guide overlay. Nothing goes to the EP-133.
- Live on Android: the **Tempo** sheet has **TEMPO** and **TIMING** tabs. TIMING holds the device's TIMING page: INTERVAL (1/1 to 1/32 with the triplets, 1/16 by default), QUANTIZE or FREE TIME, SWING (50 to 75%, at 1/8 and 1/16 only; a note says so at the others), then the arp's ORDER (as played, up, down, up and down, random), OCTAVES (1 to 3), GATE (10 to 100% of a step) and LATCH. The Pattern sheet's TIMING lists the same intervals and stays in step (Off is free time). Kept with the settings.
- Live on Android: **FX** on the phone's engine, as the EP-133 has them. A fourth function key, **FX** (PAGE), opens the FX sheet: **EFFECT** picks one master effect (delay in time with the tempo, reverb, distortion, chorus, filter, compressor; the one on, tapped again, turns off), an XY pad sets its two knobs, and a send fader per group A–D sends into it; **OUTPUT** has a compressor on the whole output and a **sidechain** (a source pad ducking the groups picked, with its length and shape). Over both pages, the pad played last sits as a cap, as in the pad sheet: held, it plays through the effects, so a setting is heard while it is made; the first effect put on in a project with no sends gives that pad's group a 50% send, and the cap says when the pad would play dry. **FX held** turns the one-group pads into the twelve punch-ins by their printed labels (repeat, tape stop, LPF, HPF, stutter and the rest), deep by pressure or by height on the pad, several at once, all let go with FX; the display line names them. Each project keeps its own FX in arc (`fx.json`); nothing is sent to the EP-133, and nothing goes into patterns. The Kotlin, C++ and web mixers render the effects alike, sample for sample, checked by shared vectors; with the FX at their defaults the output is unchanged. The web has the engine and the text in its core, with no FX screens yet.
- Live on Android: **function keys**, three two-tier keys printed as the EP-133's (a dark cap with its word on top, the lower half coloured with a second word, under an LED and a label): **SOUND** (EDIT), **PROJECT** (1–9) and **TEMPO** (TAP). A row over the pads or KEYS' grid on a phone held upright, over the groups on the all-groups and tablet pages and over a tablet's piano; on a phone on its side a column left of the pads or the grid, which closes up, shrinks its caps and in the shortest windows drops its LED lines (a screen reader still hears them). Each key ticks under a finger when Haptics is on, has its own tag in the guide overlay, and reads as a button or a switch with its state.
- **SOUND** is EDIT's key: a tap turns EDIT on or off (from KEYS it goes back to the pads and turns EDIT on), its LED lit and its label reading EDIT while it is on; a hold opens the sheet of the pad played last (with none yet, a message asks for one). Offline with nothing to give a pad, a tap says so, as the tab did.
- **PROJECT** steps to the next project, 1 → … → 9 → 1, its label saying which (P3). Held, its light comes on and a pad printed 1 to 9 (or a KEYS key in its place) goes straight to that project, as on the EP-133, while '.', 0 and ENTER stay still; let go after a long hold without one and it opens the **Project** sheet: projects 1 to 9 as dark keys in the keypad's order (7 8 9 over 4 5 6 over 1 2 3), the one shown orange; a tap goes straight there, the same way as stepping (offline, only the projects arc can show are offered). Connected, the EP-133 switches project (the same `{"active"}` write as a restore), reads it back and Live follows with its pads, the saved read and the background copies; taps while it switches pile up and the newest is written, EDIT waits meanwhile (**The EP-133 is switching projects**), and a failure says why and leaves Live on the project it read. Offline with the factory pack saved, it steps between the last read's project and the pack's projects with pads, in arc only, the display's note naming the factory project shown and EDIT's lists starting with that view's own sounds; without the pack it is greyed out and says what it needs. Greyed out too while Live reads or another action holds the device.
- **TEMPO** is a click on the phone: a tap turns it on or off, a hold opens the **Tempo** sheet (the tempo large, − and + that repeat while held, a big TAP pad for tap tempo, 40–240 BPM, and the click's switch). The click is its own small low-latency output, sample-accurate, accented on each bar's first beat, sharing Live's audio focus (a call stops it) and left out of REC's takes. While the EP-133 sends MIDI clock the click and the label follow the device's tempo, and after a Start (or Continue) its beat and bar from the next beat on, the tempo held over the Start so the click never falls back to the phone's tempo meanwhile; − + and TAP rest and the sheet says it follows. The LED blinks on each beat as it is heard, with or without the click. The phone's tempo is kept (only once chosen); the click turns off when Live is left.

- Live on Android: **a pad's sound settings in EDIT**, as the EP-133's SOUND EDIT pages have them. A pad's sheet (a tap in EDIT, or SOUND held) shows a row of knobs drawn as the K.O. II's: **PITCH** (±12 semitones, slowly by tenths) and **LEVEL** always, then the orange X and black Y knobs of the page picked under them: **SOUND** (play mode ONESHOT / KEY / LEGATO, pan), **TRIM** (start and length, over the sound's waveform with the part played lit), **ENV** (attack, release; release rests in ONESHOT, which plays to the end), **MIDI** (channel) and **MUTE** (mute group). A drag turns a knob, a double tap resets it, Haptics ticks each step, and a screen reader can set each one. Connected, the sheet asks the EP-133 for the pad's settings (else what its pad record holds, else the defaults), and turns go on the device once they rest, the whole record each time; a failure says why and goes back. If the device doesn't answer the read, the knobs rest rather than turn from a guess. Offline they are kept in arc, ask to be written with the offline pad changes, and go with **Reset pads**; written, only what was turned changes, over the pad's settings as the device has them then, and only on a pad still holding the sound they were turned for. A new sound on a pad starts its settings again from the sample, as on the device. The sound list folds away under **Change sound**, and the pad's cap at the top plays the pad while held, with its settings. A knob takes the finger from its first touch, so dragging one never scrolls the sheet.
- Live on Android plays pads with their settings: pitch, level, pan, trim, attack and release (10 ms a tick, a guess until checked on a device), ONESHOT playing to the end, KEY stacking a pad's voices, LEGATO carrying on from where the sound was, and a mute group cutting the others in it. Pads whose settings arc doesn't know yet play as before. The native engine and AudioTrack render the same samples, checked against the Kotlin mixer as before.
- Live on Android: **SAMPLE**, recording into a pad as the EP-133's sampler does. Tap the mic key in the top bar (on Live only, before **?** and the gear; from KEYS it goes to PADS in the same tap): the dark display line grows into the SAMPLE panel over the function keys' place in about 300 ms: its words fade out and SAMPLE's header fades in in their place in the first 120 ms, never the two over each other, its dark shape grows down to hold a wave strip, and a pale plate with the controls unrolls under it as one rounded body, the wave and then the rows of controls fading in, the keys fading away and the pads gliding down (shrinking beside it, on its side) to make room, and the mic key turns orange while it is open. On the all-groups page scrolled down, the page goes back up so it unrolls in sight. The mic key again, a swipe on the pads, or Back rolls it back up in about 240 ms, with a light tick each way; a swipe from right to left on the pads opens it too. Nothing hangs between the function keys and the pads, or under the open panel, where the pads sit at the page's usual gap; on a phone narrower than 400 dp, and on a small phone on its side with the display line in the top bar, the top bar's keys close up in their two groups to fit the mic key, and beside the display line come down a size and close up to it, so the line keeps the pad's sound name. The **?** guide's **Tap anywhere to close** stays under the top bar. The motion lays the pads out once per opening or closing rather than on every frame (their print settling into its new size with a short fade), and the panel composes before its clock starts, so it doesn't stutter. Opening it stops TEMPO's click, whose key it covers (the phone's speaker would play into a MIC take); BARS' count-in still clicks its bar. The **?** guide tags the mic key. A pad under the swiping finger sounds at once and is cut short once the move is a swipe, and the all-groups page still scrolls. The panel is in PADS on every layout, upright or on its side; with Android's animations off it opens and closes at once. While it is open the line's own row, still dark, is SAMPLE's header and the only status: there is no separate orange line, and the panel has no display of its own repeating it. The header holds an orange light, the source, a meter with the threshold's mark and a clip light, and what happens next (the time against the longest take while one records); TalkBack hears it as the SAMPLE pane. Under it the wave strip shows the input going by or the last take's waveform (before the first take, how long one can be); upright, it gives way before the pads get narrower than a finger, then the knobs shrink and the controls go into one row. On its side with the line on the page, the line first narrows to the panel's width while the pads come aside under it, then grows down the column's left as they rise beside it, so neither is ever over the other; with the line in the top bar, the bar's line fades through to the header in step with the panel (and back as it closes) and the panel under it has none of its own; on its side the wave strip takes the column's height the controls (and the USB note) leave, so the plate under it is never empty. Opening the panel on the all-groups page leaves the offline note unfolded or folded as it was. The status keeps to one line, smaller where the header is narrow (there the source is left to the plate's source window under it); in a short window on its side the knobs' words come down a size, and on a tablet the controls keep together in the plate's middle; the page's pads record, empty ones blinking and those with a sound lit. The controls are −/+ through MIC, MIC ST, RSP, RSP ST, USB and USB ST, STEREO, KNOB X LEVEL (−12 to +30 dB, kept per source), KNOB Y THRESH (Off, or −60 to 0 dB, keeping the 20 ms before the sound), BARS (Free, 1–16) and LATCH. MIC is the phone's mic, as raw as it comes. RSP is Live's own mix without the click, pads played meanwhile included, beside REC if it runs. USB (experimental) is the EP-133's sound over USB audio on OS 2.5; if it goes, the take so far is kept and RSP stands in. Hold a pad to record (a quick tap plays a pad that has a sound instead). With LATCH on a tap records hands-free, and LATCH reads STOP to stop it, as does a tap on the pad. With BARS set, a hands-free take lasts that many bars after a one-bar count-in on the click, or from USB with the EP-133's clock it waits for its PLAY. Takes stop by themselves at 20 s in stereo or 40 s in mono (OS 2.5); **Disk low** shows, and takes stop sooner, when the device's free space can't hold a full one. Each take opens a **New sample** sheet: the waveform, START and LENGTH knobs, play, Normalize, Trim silence, the slot (the next free one, − and + over the free slots), then KEEP, RETAKE or DISCARD with UNDO. Settings → Live → **Review samples** off puts takes straight on their pad. KEEP puts the take on its pad at once, playing from the phone with fresh settings. Connected it uploads in the background (the progress in SAMPLE's header), and a failed upload is offered again at once. Offline it waits for the connect question (**Put 1 new sample on the EP-133?**) and goes into the next free slot on Write. No recording is lost: one with no pad to go on, one replaced, reset or discarded before it reached the device, and one arc was closed on all go to Live tools → Takes. Closing the panel, leaving Live or the app stops a take and keeps it. The microphone is asked for on the first open with MIC or USB; refused, RSP stands in, with a key to arc's settings once Android stops asking. Screen readers get the mic key as Sample, On or Off, each pad's state, and a pad click that latches, stops or cancels a hands-free take. Live tools' note on how Live reads the EP-133 now says KEEP writes to it. The web has the sampling logic and text in its core only, with the same tests.

- Live on Android: **patterns**, the EP-133's RECORD and PLAY on the phone: the pads and KEYS notes played go into a looping pattern that plays back, online and offline. A **● RECORD** and a **▶ PLAY** chip start Live's display line (glyphs alone in the top bar's line on a phone on its side, a row of their own on the all-groups display). A tap on RECORD arms it (its light blinks; the line says what starts it, and the grid), and PLAY then counts a bar in on the click (four beats, the first accented, a big digit on the line, **3 / 4**, and a haptic tick each with Haptics on) and records from bar 1; RECORD and PLAY together start at once, as does PLAY with **COUNT-IN** off. While it plays, a tap on RECORD punches in or out, RECORD held records only while held, and PLAY (■ meanwhile) stops. Passes overdub, a note on the same pad and pitch at the same step replacing the old one. Notes snap to **TIMING** (Off, 1/8, 1/16 as on the device, 1/32), a note the grid moves later than it was heard isn't played again in that pass, and each keeps its gate (from the touch to the lift: the pads' and keys' lifts now carry their own touch time) and, from KEYS, its pitch. Running, the line counts bar and beat of the group played last (**2.3 / 4**, and the grid while recording), with a hairline along its foot going round the loop and a signal border while recording; the EP-133's own ▶/■ steps aside meanwhile. Holding RECORD opens the **Pattern** sheet: each group's **LENGTH**, 1 to 99 bars (− and + that repeat while held, 1, 2, 4 and 8 at a tap, and **×2**, doubling it with its notes copied in, as SHIFT + + does), **AUTO** length (an empty group recorded from stop ends where recording stops, at 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars), TIMING, COUNT-IN, UNDO, ERASE, and **CLEAR** for a group or all of them, asked first. Groups loop at their own lengths. **ERASE** latches, from the sheet or a chip on the line (while the project has notes and the pattern plays, where it fits; on a tablet's wide line while stopped too): pads with notes get a dot, a tap erases a pad's notes, and a pad held while the pattern plays erases its notes as the playhead passes; in KEYS a key erases that note; ERASE and EDIT turn each other off, and leaving Live turns ERASE off. **↶ UNDO** (SHIFT + B on the device), in the sheet and beside ERASE on the line, takes back the last pass recorded, erase, clear or length change, up to 32 steps per project while arc runs. Patterns play on the phone, whatever sound is on each pad, into Live's mix to the frame (scheduled 50 ms ahead on the output's own thread); the pads ring and the KEYS keys outline as they play. Pads whose sounds aren't on the phone yet load quietly from arc's copies or a backup, counted on the line and in the sheet meanwhile. The tempo is TEMPO's (the EP-133's while it sends MIDI clock), a change mid-play stays in time, and the click follows the pattern's bars while it runs. A call, leaving Live or leaving arc stops it, recording kept. Patterns are saved per project in arc's own files (`patterns.json`, not Documents/arc) and never go on the EP-133; TIMING, COUNT-IN and AUTO are kept with the settings in library.json. Screen readers hear RECORD and PLAY as buttons with their state (**Record, armed**, **Play, bar 2 of 4**) and each change of the transport once. Not yet tried on a phone.
- Live on Android: SAMPLE's **BARS** goes on past 16 to **PTN** while the project has pattern notes: a hands-free take as long as the longest group's pattern, from its start, to resample it onto a pad. Stopped, the patterns start for it (playing, not recording) and the take with bar 1; playing, it starts at the next loop. While the patterns play, a BARS take of 1 to 16 bars starts on the next bar with no count-in. RSP's take starts at that exact frame of Live's mix; MIC's and USB's as the bar is heard. Opening SAMPLE stops a pattern recording, and PLAY goes on.
- Live on Android: with RECORD armed, **the first pad played starts recording**, as on the EP-133: no PLAY needed, bar 1 begins on that press (heard where it was played, through Live's output clock, which runs while RECORD is armed), and the press is the pattern's first note, sounding once. A KEYS note does the same, and so does a pad held in EDIT (which plays it); SAMPLE's and ERASE's pads, EDIT's tap and the pad sheet's cap don't. PLAY still counts a bar in. The armed line says **Play a pad or PLAY · 1/16**.
- Web: the pattern's logic and text in its core, ported with the same tests (the model and its JSON, recording with TIMING, overdub, gates, AUTO length, ERASE, CLEAR, lengths, ×2 and UNDO, the transport, and the sequencer's windows); no RECORD or PLAY on its screens yet.

### Changed
- Android: **hold to disconnect**. The connection key in the top bar no longer disconnects on a tap while the EP-133 is connected: a tap shows **Hold to disconnect** as a toast with a light tick, and holding it for a second disconnects. A ring fills round the key's edge, clockwise from the top, as the hold goes, and a confirming tick comes when it is done; letting go early, or sliding off the key, cancels it and the ring starts over. Not connected, a tap connects at once as before. Screen readers keep the key's name (now **EP-133 connected: hold to disconnect**) and get **Disconnect** as an action and as its long click, with no timed hold; the **?** guide tags it **Connection: hold to disconnect**. With Android's animations off the hold still takes a second but the ring doesn't fill. The ticks follow Settings → Haptics. The hold's timing is a small unit of its own with tests (a second, early release cancels). The web's key still disconnects on a tap; its text gains the new sentences (`HOLD_TO_DISCONNECT`, `CONNECTED_HOLD`, `CONNECTION_HOLD`, `DISCONNECT`) for Android's twin.
- Live on Android: **bigger pads on tall phones**. Held upright, one group's pads and KEYS' grid used to stop at the width and leave spare height above and below the body on a tall phone (about 412 x 843 dp and up). The pads now grow into it, taller than wide, to at most 1.15 times as high as wide (a pad is 0.93 times as high as wide otherwise); the width, the gaps and the group and function keys stay where they were. A small phone (360 x 640 dp), where the height is what limits the pads, and a phone on its side are unchanged. The sizing is a function of its own (`padFit`) with tests (a tall phone grows, a small one doesn't, the cap holds), and the SAMPLE, STEP and SCENE panels still glide the pads in and out.
- Android: **a slimmer top bar**. The orange **●** (back up) and the settings gear leave the top bar, which keeps the section tag, the connection key, Live's mic key and **?**, so the keys no longer close up on a 360 dp phone or beside the display line. Back up is the orange block first in the **Backups** caption row, before search and import (while there are no backups the big **Back up device** key stays). **Settings** is the first row of **Live tools** (a gear, the word and a chevron, which closes the tools and opens Settings) and the last entry of the section list the tag opens, set apart from the sections. The guide overlay tags both new places, and drops the two it no longer has in the top bar.
- Live: the one-group display line and the KEYS line no longer show **Offline** or the project number (**P3**), so the sound's name has room; the top bar's connection icon and the PROJECT key show them, and screen readers still hear both. The all-groups display keeps its **Offline ▾**. On Android and the web.
- Live on Android: **REC is now TAKE**, as RECORD is the pattern's on the device, and moves off the display line into **Live tools → Takes**: a **● TAKE** key over the list starts and stops a take, its time on it while it records. While a take is armed or records, a small **● TAKE 0:12** badge sits on the display line (in the top bar on a phone on its side); a tap on it stops it. Takes record, save and play as before.
- Live's mixers take timed commands: a start or a release at a given frame inside a block, a release of one voice only by its tag (so a pattern's gate never cuts the same pad held by a finger), and dropping every pending one. Kotlin, C++ and TypeScript behave alike, checked by the shared golden vectors, with timed scenarios appended (inside a block, at its edge, late, against a mute group, random ones at 44.1 and 48 kHz and blocks of 1 to 1024 frames); with no timed commands the output is the same, sample for sample. The native engine's queue carries them with no allocation on the audio thread, and its output tells how far it has rendered.
