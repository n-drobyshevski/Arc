# Changelog

All notable changes to arc (Android and web). Versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/):
a major version breaks `.pak` or library compatibility, a minor version adds features, a patch
version fixes bugs. The version itself is set in `version.properties`.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Android: **the beat card sheet's switches are LED keys.** SET CHAIN, the tempo's SET, APPLY FX and PUT ON PADS were navy or flat pills that read like buttons; each is now a key with a light, orange and marked **ON** while it is on, dark and marked **OFF** while not. The tempo row says what will happen: **136 → 85** while SET is on, **136 · card 85** while it is off.
- Android and web: with the **EP-40 Riddim** kit picked, the sound lists (a pad's CHANGE SOUND, PICK SOUND on the beat card sheet) and the Device tab name the hundreds past 599 as that pack fills them: **600–699 CHORDS**, **700–799 VOX · FX**, **800–899 LOOPS**, under the range like **500–599 MELODIC**. - Android and web: **shares to Claude name the factory kit.** When the kit picked in Arc isn't the EP-133's, a beat share (a pattern, a scene or the chain) says so in one sentence between its request and the card: *My EP-133 plays the EP-40 Riddim factory kit.* (or *the EP-1320 Medieval factory kit.*), with or without the sound list. **The arc-beats skill knows the EP-40 Riddim kit on an EP-133**: a new reference, `ep40-kit.md`, gives the pack's slot blocks (kicks, snares and rims, hats, percussion, bass, keys and horns, chord stabs 600-628, shouts and dub FX 700-754, loops 800-898), each producer's loops with their tempo and length in bars, P01's pads as Arc's Live lays them out (kick A1, rimshot A2, hats A4/A5, drum loops A7-A9, bass and melody loops on B and C, sirens and FX on D; the pads on the EP-40's own synth voices are silent on an EP-133) and the loop rules (the card's tempo is the loop's; strike a loop once a pass). `genres.md` gains seven recipes for the kit: one drop, rockers, steppers, dub, digital riddim, dancehall and UK dub. The script notices the kit from the sentence or the pack's names (or `--kit ep40`), reads roles by its blocks, and `check` warns when a loop's tempo isn't the card's. SKILL.md and the zip are updated.

Six keys in a row where the panel is wide enough, two rows of three where not. TalkBack reads the picks as choices and SOUNDS as a switch. The links and **About beat cards** stay below. ## [0.4.0] - 2026-10-18

### Added
- Live on Android: **a new Live tools sheet**, a front page and four pages. The front page opens on a dark **now display**: while a take records, its time large in orange with a **STOP** key (armed, **Waiting for the first sound** and a **● TAKE** key that calls it off); after a share to Claude, **Shared to Claude** with what went (**Scene S02**, **A · 01**, **Chain**), **Paste Claude's reply when it's ready**, **PASTE BEAT** and a **×** to stop waiting (a card arriving or its import ends it too); otherwise the group or key (**Group A · Follow**, **Keys · DO major**), the project and the last thing the EP-133 played, a KEYS note on two octaves of piano or the pad hit. Under it one plate of rows, each with a live line, opens a page: **Play** (the view switch, **Follow**, **Mode**, note repeat; in KEYS the key and the colours), **Claude** (the beat cards, with the notes folded under **About beat cards**), **Takes** (the takes, with their hints under **About takes**) and **Help** (how Live reads the EP-133, or how Keys works, in the open). A page slides in with **‹ Play** as its head, Back and the **‹** return to the front page, and the sheet opens on the front page each time. The factory sounds and **Reset pads** became tinted notices above the display, and Settings a gear key before the close key. The web's panel is unchanged.
- Live on Android: **scene chains.** The SCENE panel gets a **CHAIN** row under the group columns: an **ON** chip, the steps as chips (**S03 ×2 → S04 → …**, a tap cycles the passes ×1 ×2 ×4 ×8, a long press removes the step, a dashed **＋ S03** adds the scene shown, **↻** at the end) and, while it plays, **S03 · PASS 1/2** in orange with the step lit and a thin progress bar. With the chain on, each scene plays its passes and the next step's scene takes over sample-accurately at the end of its longest pattern, looping at the end; PLAY starts at the first step with the scene showing, STOP keeps the chain and resets its passes, and choosing a scene or pattern by hand (or COMMIT) turns it off (**Chain off: you picked a scene.**). Edits are one UNDO step each and are saved with the project (`patterns.json` version 3, which still reads versions 1 and 2). A chain has a guide tag.
- Live on Android: **beat cards of several scenes.** A card can hold `scene` sections and a `chain` line (a whole track idea: intro, drop, breakdown, drop; the format is in `skill/arc-beats/references/beat-card.md`). **SHARE CHAIN · N SCENES** in the CLAUDE card (when the project has a chain) sends the chain as such a card; the import sheet lists each scene's grids and where it goes (**Scene 1 · Intro** → **new S02 · A 01 · B (same)**) and the card's chain with a **SET CHAIN** chip (on by default). Importing adds a scene for each card scene and sets the chain, in one UNDO step with the rest. The arc-beats skill writes, analyses (energy per scene, the arrangement) and renders such cards.
- Live on Android: **beat cards flag silent pads.** A card that plays a pad with no sound on your EP-133 (a melody on D7 when group D is empty) used to import silent. The import sheet now has an amber **SILENT PADS** block before SOUNDS (**D7 · 12 notes, no sound: they will be silent**) with **PICK SOUND**, which opens the pad sheet's sound list (search, hundreds, preview; the EP-133's sounds while connected, the offline list otherwise). A pick adds a ticked row to SOUNDS (**No sound → 512 PIANO**) and the silent row says **→ 512 PIANO** with **CHANGE**; IMPORT puts it on the pad with the card's other sounds, in the same step as the card. UNDO takes the patterns back; connected, the pad keeps the picked sound (a pad that had none can't be emptied again), offline UNDO drops it, and the SOUNDS note says so. The block shows only once Live has read the pads. A share, when Arc knows the pads, carries a comment after the header naming them (`# no sound on: C7 D7`), which the skill's script reports as `silent_pads` and the skill's BUILD and ANALYSE steps use. The core and its web twin have `silentPads` with the same tests.
- Live on Android: **beat cards carry FX and pad shaping.** A card can set the project's FX (`fx`, `send`, `comp`, `sidechain` lines) and shape pads (`pad A9 pitch 2 release 40 mode key`; the format is in `skill/arc-beats/references/beat-card.md`). The import sheet gets an **FX** block before SOUNDS (a row each for **EFFECT**, **SENDS**, **COMP** and **DUCK**, old struck through and new after the arrow, **Already set** when equal, an **APPLY FX** chip on by default) and a **PAD SHAPING** block after it (a ticked row per pad, **pitch 0 → +2 · release 255 → 40**), with notes saying where each goes. IMPORT applies the FX through the mixer and `fx.json`, then the new sounds, then the ticked pads' settings (written to the EP-133 while connected, else kept as offline pad settings), all as one UNDO step: UNDO puts the old FX back unless they were changed since, and the old pad settings back except on a pad whose sound the import changed (a new sound resets them). A share now writes the project's FX and each used pad's settings as the same lines, and its first line asks Claude to keep the sound, FX and pad lines. The core, the web twin, the skill and its script read and write them with the same tests.
- Live on Android: **beat cards**, a beat as plain text that Claude can read, analyse and write. A **CLAUDE** section in Live tools (after the view settings, before TAKES, on every layout) has a **Beat cards** card with **SHARE SCENE S02** (the scene playing, its blank patterns left out), **SHARE A · 01** (the playing pattern of the group shown) and **PASTE BEAT**; the share keys are dimmed, and still announced, when there are no notes. A share sends the system share sheet a line asking Claude to analyse the beat with the arc-beats skill, then the ARC BEAT card in a fenced block (the sounds' names, Live's tempo, TIMING's swing where every hit sits on it, velocities and short gates rounded). **PASTE BEAT** reads the clipboard, and Claude's reply shared to arc from the Claude app opens Live with the same sheet (a text with no ARC BEAT line toasts **No beat card in that text.**). The **beat card sheet** shows the card's name and size, a read-only step grid for each group (a row for each pad, cells lit by velocity; the first two bars of a long pattern and **+N bars**), where each group goes (its next free pattern, **A · 04**) and the new scene a card of several groups adds, the card's tempo as a chip (**SET · NOW 122**, off until chosen), its swing, and its mistakes with their lines (**COPY PROBLEMS** copies them for Claude). **IMPORT** never overwrites a pattern, takes over at once, and is one UNDO step; it is off, with the reason, when the card can't be read or a group has no free pattern. Under the card, **Get the arc-beats skill** opens its zip and **Learn with Claude** shares a starter prompt. An info key after CLAUDE says how to add the skill in the Claude app or Claude Code. Works offline; nothing goes to the EP-133. The web has the card's text and `hasCard`, with the same tests, but no screens.
- Live on Android: **beat cards choose sounds.** A card can say which sound each pad plays with a line `sound A7 12 Micro kick` (a slot 1 to 999 and, optionally, the sound's name, which Arc checks the slot still holds; the format is in `skill/arc-beats/references/beat-card.md`). A share now writes a sound line for every pad its notes use, and **With my sound list · 212 sounds**, a tick box under the two share keys (on by default, kept like the other Live preferences, hidden when Arc knows no sounds), adds after the card the sounds Arc knows (the EP-133's while connected, else the last read's or the factory pack's) so Claude can choose from them; the share's first line now asks Claude to keep the sound lines or choose from that list. The beat card sheet gets a **SOUNDS** block after the Goes to rows when the card has sound lines: a row for each, with a tick box (ticked by default), the pad, the pad's old sound struck through and the new one (**012 MICRO KICK**); a pad that plays the sound already says **Already there**, and a sound that is not in the list is amber with the reason (**Not on your EP-133: 301 RIM DUSTY**), both without a box; a slot that no longer holds the named sound is found by its name. **PUT ON PADS** switches them all, and a note says what IMPORT will do: connected, it writes the ticked sounds to the active project's pads on the EP-133 (their pitch, level and other settings reset to the sound's), offline it saves them as offline pad changes that go to the EP-133 when it connects. IMPORT puts the patterns in first, then the sounds, one pad after another; a pad Arc has no target for is skipped and counted in the toast (**Imported to A · 04 and 2 sounds. UNDO takes it back.**), and a write that fails says why while the patterns stay. The toast's **Undo** is the same one-step UNDO as ↶ and SHIFT + B: it takes back the patterns and writes the pads' old sounds again (offline, it restores the previous offline changes and settings), as long as the import is the last edit; after other edits UNDO takes those back first, and the pads change back with the import itself. A pad the import found empty can't be emptied again. The core, the web twin and the arc-beats skill read, write and match the sound lines with the same tests.
- Live on Android: **beat cards and factory sounds without names.** A slot the EP-133 lists as `200.pcm` takes the factory pack's name in the shared sound list and in a card's sound lines (when the pack is saved); the list says what the slot ranges are when a name is still like that, a sound line for such a slot is used by its slot (the sheet row says **Card says HH CLOSED · name not checked**), and a card none of whose sounds are on your EP-133 hints to share with the sound list. The skill, the core and the web twin do the same.
- Live on Android: **Make up for Bluetooth delay**, a switch in Settings → Live (on by default; its info key says what it does and the delay now). While the sound goes to Bluetooth or a hearing aid, Live reads the output's own latency about once a second (Oboe's estimate on the native engine, the frames in flight by the output's timestamp on AudioTrack) and makes up the part of a Bluetooth delay that the output's clock leaves out: a wireless output is taken to delay 180 ms at least, less what the measured latency already counts, and all of it when nothing can be measured. The pattern's clock is arc's own, so its notes are not sent earlier; the playhead, the count-in and TEMPO's light follow what is heard, and a pad or key recorded into a pattern is placed the delay before it was made (a press just before the loop comes round lands on the beat, one early in a pass at the end of the pass before; counted in, a downbeat a little early is the downbeat, and in a run a pad started, a chord's other fingers land on it with the first). The click that follows the EP-133's MIDI clock is sent earlier by that delay and heard on the device's beat. The Bluetooth key's toast says the delay is made up for (**Bluetooth plays late (about 180 ms): arc makes up for it**). Wired and the speaker are unchanged, bit for bit. Nobody has played it over Bluetooth yet.
- Live on Android: while the sound goes to Bluetooth or a hearing aid, Live's top bar keeps a small amber **Bluetooth key** before the connection key, for as long as it does: square and the connection key's size, a Bluetooth glyph and a clock drawn like the other icons in dark ink on a new `warn` amber, tagged in the **?** guide. A tap shows **Bluetooth plays late: wired or the speaker is quicker** as a toast (over the bar's middle on a phone on its side, as other toasts); a long press names it (**Bluetooth delay**) and a screen reader reads the sentence. The display line stays as it was, with no chip: it says "Press a pad" or the hit. (Before, the sentence took the line's place, where a phone cut it to "Bluetoot…" and the first pad hit hid it.)
- Live on Android: **patterns, scenes and copy/paste**, the EP-133's GROUP and MAIN with − / + or a number, COMMIT, ERASE + MAIN held, the scene change setting, and SHIFT + C / D, on the phone. Each group now holds up to 99 patterns of its own length and a project up to 99 scenes (a scene is the pattern each group plays); the one pattern per group before this is pattern 1 of scene 1. An **S01** chip after ▶ on the stopped line (and, where there is room, while it plays) unrolls the line into the **SCENE panel** over the function keys, where STEP's goes; while the pattern plays and there is more than one scene, or a group is on a pattern other than 1, the line reads **S02 · A01 B03→05 C01 D02** instead, the number waiting blinking (steady with animations off). The group keys carry the patterns' numbers in the same cases. On the panel's dark screen: ▶ (■), the scene's − and + (past the last, a new scene on each group's next free pattern), four group columns with the pattern's number (a tap opens the **1–99 grid** over the pads: patterns with notes filled, the one playing orange, the next free one outlined), − and + and **NEXT FREE**; **COMMIT** (the scene copied after itself, into free slots); **CLR**, held 2 s (**DEL** on an empty scene): the scene's notes cleared or the scene deleted; **CHANGE**, cycling Immediate, Bar end and Pattern end, also in the Pattern sheet as **SCENE CHANGE**; and **CLIP**: **PTN**, **BAR** (with the focused group's bar pages) or **PAD**, **COPY** and **PASTE** (PAD: COPY, a tap on a pad, PASTE, a tap on the target, in any group). Unlike STEP's the panel stays open while the pattern plays, the pads stay playable under it (but for the PAD clip's two taps), and a pick made while playing takes over at the next bar line or at the end of the pattern, sample-accurately, the old pattern's notes ringing out (Immediate: on the spot; stopped, always at once), a group's pick replacing its own and a scene's cancelling the groups'. STOP applies what waits and UNDO drops it. COMMIT, CLR / DEL and every paste are one UNDO step, which restores the scenes and patterns shown. Patterns are saved in `patterns.json` version 2 (version 1 still reads). A pattern switched in starts at its own bar 1 on the tick it takes over, as the device starts it (a 2-bar pattern switched in on an odd bar plays its bar 1 first, and what is recorded into it, the playhead, ERASE and CORRECT held and AUTO length all count from there; Bar end and Pattern end are the pattern's own lines from where it started, a scene's Bar end the transport's); PLAY starts every pattern at bar 1 again. The clipboard stays when the project changes (until arc is closed), so a pattern, bar or pad's notes copied in one project pastes in another, and nothing goes to the EP-133. Screen readers hear the scene, each change once, the keys, the hold key's long click and every grid cell. The web has the core (the model, copy and paste, undo, the file, the text and where each pattern started) with the same tests, but no screens yet. Not yet tried on a phone.
- Live on Android: **STEP on every view of Live.** The STEP chip and panel, until now on one group's pads and KEYS' grid upright, now come with **the piano** (upright on a tablet, and on a phone's side), **all four groups** and **KEYS' grid on its side**. The chip is where each view shows the pattern's chips (the piano's and the grid's KEYS line, the top bar's line, and on the all-groups page the pattern's row under the big line); the panel is the same, with the same cursor, held RECORD, VEL and LEN, NUDGE and CORRECT. Where the line sits over function keys it grows into the panel over them (the four groups, and a tablet's piano); KEYS' grid on its side takes it in the function keys' column, and the short sideways piano, which has no function keys, in a new column left of the keys, each with a header of its own and the keys narrowing beside it (the piano keeps its range). On the piano the notes on the step are outlined on their keys, the one picked ringed, a key held while RECORD is held lit, and a long press picks a key. STEP edits the group shown: the group key selected on one group's pads, the KEYS sound's group on KEYS, and with all four groups the group last selected, then the group a pad was pressed in last (the panel goes there on the step it shows, so RECORD + a pad in another group places the note where the panel's cursor is), its caption in orange; a press that turns into a scroll on that page places nothing. Screen readers hear the group edited.
- Live on Android: **step sequencing and timing correct**, the EP-133's − / + while stopped, RECORD + pad, SHIFT + KNOB X / Y, SHIFT + pad then − / +, and SHIFT + TIMING. A **STEP** chip on the stopped display line (its strip glyph where the line is short) unrolls the line into the **STEP panel** over the function keys, as SAMPLE's panel unrolls, in every view of Live (see below). On its dark screen: the panel's held **RECORD**, ▶ and a status (**STEP 1.2.1**, **+ SNARE**, **KICK → 1.2.2**, **KICK +1 TK**, **5 CORRECTED**); − and + either side of a strip of one bar of steps at TIMING's interval (dots for notes, the cursor orange, a tap jumps, **BAR** pages a longer pattern), the notes on the step sounding as the cursor lands; **VEL** and **LEN** knobs for every note on the step (LEN from a tick to a bar; one UNDO step per turn); **NUDGE** and **CORRECT**. Hold RECORD and tap pads or KEYS keys to put them on the step, one interval long, at the touch's pressure or full velocity, the same pad and pitch replaced; without it a press only sounds, and in the panel nothing arms or goes to the arp. The pads on the cursor's step are outlined. A long press (or NUDGE and a tap) picks a pad with a note there, ringed and tagged NUDGE, for − / + to move its note a step on the swung grid (a tick in free time), the cursor following. CORRECT puts a tapped pad's notes on TIMING's grid and swing, and with a pad held − / + move all its notes a tick; while the pattern plays it stays lit on the line, on every page, and a pad held corrects its notes as they pass, counted on the line, one UNDO step a hold (two pads held together, a tap on one with another held too, are one step, from the first down to the last up). CORRECT and ERASE turn each other off, as STEP and SAMPLE (and EDIT) do. The cursors are kept per project and group while arc runs. Screen readers hear the cursor, each change once, the strip's steps, the knobs and the latches as switches, and the pads have a **Pick to nudge** action. Nothing goes to the EP-133; the web has the core (the steps, the edits and the text) with the same tests, but no screens yet.
- Live on Android: **the arp and note repeat**, the EP-133's TIMING + pads, on a switch. **ARP / RPT** sits on the pads' plate under KEYS / PADS (one group and KEYS' grid, upright, on its side and on a tablet; over the piano in its row; in Live tools with all four groups): in KEYS (**ARP**) the keys held arpeggiate, in PADS (**RPT**) every pad held repeats on each step, at TIMING's interval. It lights orange while on, and **LATCH** under it keeps the notes going after the fingers lift, until a press after every finger is up starts a new set. The notes held are outlined (KEYS numbers them in the order pressed), the one sounding is lit, and the display line says what plays: **ARP · 1/16 · DO FA LA**, **ARP ∞ · …** latched, **REPEAT · 1/16 · A 7**. Where the screen reports pressure, a held key's or pad's pressure is its velocity. With RECORD on, the arp's notes go into the pattern like played ones. Each switch ticks under the finger with Haptics on, reads as a switch with its state (LATCH, while ARP is off, says to turn it on first) and has its tag in the guide overlay. Nothing goes to the EP-133.
- Live on Android: the **Tempo** sheet has **TEMPO** and **TIMING** tabs. TIMING holds the device's TIMING page: INTERVAL (1/1 to 1/32 with the triplets, 1/16 by default), QUANTIZE or FREE TIME, SWING (50 to 75%, at 1/8 and 1/16 only; a note says so at the others), then the arp's ORDER (as played, up, down, up and down, random), OCTAVES (1 to 3), GATE (10 to 100% of a step) and LATCH. The Pattern sheet's TIMING lists the same intervals and stays in step (Off is free time). Kept with the settings.
- Live on Android: **FX** on the phone's engine, as the EP-133 has them. A fourth function key, **FX** (PAGE), opens the FX sheet: **EFFECT** picks one master effect (delay in time with the tempo, reverb, distortion, chorus, filter, compressor; the one on, tapped again, turns off), an XY pad sets its two knobs, and a send fader per group A–D sends into it; **OUTPUT** has a compressor on the whole output and a **sidechain** (a source pad ducking the groups picked, with its length and shape). Over both pages, the pad played last sits as a cap, as in the pad sheet: held, it plays through the effects, so a setting is heard while it is made; the first effect put on in a project with no sends gives that pad's group a 50% send, and the cap says when the pad would play dry. **FX held** turns the one-group pads into the twelve punch-ins by their printed labels (repeat, tape stop, LPF, HPF, stutter and the rest), deep by pressure or by height on the pad, several at once, all let go with FX; the display line names them. Each project keeps its own FX in arc (`fx.json`); nothing is sent to the EP-133, and nothing goes into patterns. The Kotlin, C++ and web mixers render the effects alike, sample for sample, checked by shared vectors; with the FX at their defaults the output is unchanged.
