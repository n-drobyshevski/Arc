# arc for EP-133 K.O. II (Android and web)

A free, open-source backup librarian for the teenage engineering EP-133 K.O. II, as a native Android app. It talks to the device over USB-C with Android's MIDI API (`android.media.midi`). No account, no server, nothing leaves your phone unless you share it. The same app also runs in the browser: see [Web app](#web-app).

This is a port of the web version in [`reference/`](reference/), which is kept read only as the spec. The two behave the same and their `.pak` files are interchangeable. [`web/`](web/) is a port of this Android app back to the browser, with every feature below; it also reads and writes the same `.pak` files.

The look keeps the web version's device panel, keys and orange signal, and borrows its layout from teenage engineering's pocket operator app. There is no bar along the bottom. The top left holds a tag naming the section, like the PO app's EDIT tag; tap it for **Backups**, **Live** or **Device** (long-press opens the debug screen). The EP-133 shortcut guide is a **GUIDE** tab on the left edge, like the PO's TUTORIAL tab, that slides the guide in over the page. The controls are icons, as on the PO's top row. The top bar holds the section tag, then an orange **●** (back up), the connection key (green with a dot while connected, where a tap disconnects; navy with a ring when not), **?** and the settings gear. Long-press any icon for its name; screen readers read it too. **?** opens a guide overlay, shown once by itself on the first start: the page fades and every control on screen gets a coloured tag with an arrow, like the PO app's tutorial. View switches are quiet words with an underline. Every key is drawn as the official Sample Tool draws the K.O. II: a flat face over a flat edge offset down and to the right, and pressed, the face travels onto its edge; a choice that is on stays down. Live's pads are dark keys sitting in the device's grey body, labelled top left as on the device, over pale A–D keys with an LED above each (the group shown stays down with its LED lit). The landscape piano's keys are the same: white and black keys standing in the grey body that go down under your finger, with a light haptic tick for each key you hit or slide onto. The page is cream with navy ink, labels are uppercase, and lists sit on pale plates split by thin lines. arc opens on **Live**, and Back on any other section returns to it.

The whole app turns with the phone, following Android's auto-rotate (with auto-rotate off, tap the rotate button Android shows). Turning doesn't restart anything: Live keeps its read of the device, its lights and the notes you hold fade out. On its side the top bar slims down and takes Live's display line and the messages in its middle, so they never cover the keys (in a window too narrow for that, such as a split screen, they stay on the page and at the bottom); Backups shows the device as one line above the list, and the trim view lays its keys beside the waveform.

## What it does

- Back up every sound and project on the device to a `.pak` file stored on your phone
- Restore a whole backup, or only some projects plus the sounds they use (optionally also the sounds no project uses)
- Keep a library of backups with names and notes
- Share a backup through the Android share sheet, or save the `.pak` file anywhere with the system file picker
- Import `.pak` backups made by the official Sample Tool, by the web version or by a friend, and open them by tapping a `.pak` in the Files app
- **Survive reinstalling arc:** every backup is also written to **Documents/arc**, with a `library.json` that keeps titles, notes, dates and arc's config: the settings you chose, Live's learned pad names and pad order, and whether the guide overlay was shown. Live's last read of the device goes there too, as `live.json` (its offline pad changes, `live-pads.json`, its patterns, `patterns.json`, and each project's FX, `fx.json`, stay in the app's own files). Edits update the copy, and deleting a backup in arc deletes its file there too.
  - After a reinstall, tap **Restore from Documents/arc**: on the empty library, or under the list until the folder has been picked. Android makes you pick the folder once; the picker opens there. Only settings you changed are written, so using a fresh install before restoring never resets the old ones, and pads learned in the meantime are kept alongside the restored ones.
    - Only Documents/arc, or a folder that already holds arc backups, is taken as the library. Any other folder is refused.
    - Backups already in arc are skipped.
  - On every start, anything missing from the folder is copied there: a library from before this version, or a copy that failed earlier. A failed copy is reported with the result of the save or delete.
  - Google's automatic app backup is not used, because it is capped well below the size of one backup.
- Keep running when the phone is locked: transfers run in a foreground service with a progress notification and a Cancel action

These go beyond the web version:
- **Browse the device:** the **Device** tab shows the free space in large type over a 24-segment meter split into sounds, projects and free, and switches between **Sounds** and **Projects**. Sounds are grouped by hundreds of slots (001–099 Kicks, 100–199 Snares, …, under an orange bar with the count), can be found by name or slot number, and a row of range keys pinned at the top jumps between the groups. Each dense row has a round **Play** key that plays the sound on the phone in one tap, downloading it only when arc holds no copy; tap a row for its channels, sample rate, play mode and checksum as chips. Projects are the nine slots as K.O. II pad keys (7 8 9 / 4 5 6 / 1 2 3): a project that exists is a dark key with its size, an empty slot is outlined, and the one picked is orange; under them, its size, its sounds by name, and its **Pads**. A wide window (840 dp and up, not short; 1024px on the web) shows both at once: storage and projects in a column on the left, the sounds as a binder on the right with tabs for each range, rows of the picked project's sounds badged (P3), and an **All / In P3** filter.
- **Add samples:** pick WAV files on the phone and load them into sample slots. Each file gets the next free slot, which you can change; an occupied slot is replaced. Uploads take exactly the restore path, so they get the same free-space check, resampling above 46875 Hz, checksum verification and Cancel.
- **Compare with device:** in the restore sheet, see what a restore would change before running it: which sounds differ, are missing or have other settings, which projects differ, and what on the device the restore leaves alone. Nothing is written.
- **Backup contents:** tap **Contents** in a backup's sheet to list its sounds and projects. No device is needed.
  - Sounds play on the phone, and share or save as WAV files exactly as stored in the backup.
  - Each project shares or saves as its own `.pak` with only the sounds it uses, in the same layout, so it restores like any other backup.
- **Trim before upload:** each file picked under **Add samples** has a **Trim** key. It opens a waveform where you set the start and end, hear the selection, and reset. The cut happens at upload time, at the file's own sample rate, and loop points embedded in the WAV move with the new start.
- **Play from the device:** in the device browser, a sound's details have a **Play** key that plays the sound on the phone. It plays from Live's samples in memory or arc's pad sound copy when they hold that sound (the copy only while it still matches the device), and downloads it otherwise; a download plays first and is copied afterwards. Sounds from a backup or a take are kept decoded for a while, so playing one again starts at once.
- **Pad layout:** a **Pads** key on a project, in a backup's contents or on the device, shows each group's pads with the sound on each. In a backup, tapping a pad plays its sound. Pads are listed by their number in the project file; how those numbers map to the physical pads isn't known, so the grid doesn't claim to match the device's layout.
- **Search sounds:** a **Search** key next to **Import** finds sounds by name in every saved backup. Tapping a result opens that backup's contents.
- **Compare two backups:** **Compare with another backup** in a backup's sheet shows what changed from the older one to the newer one: sounds added, removed or changed (audio, name or settings) and projects added, removed or changed, with the pads that moved. Audio counts as the same when the samples are the same, even in a differently written WAV file.
- **Live mirror:** the **Live** tab shows the EP-133 as you play it, either all four groups or **one group** at a time as a large grid with A–D keys under it (lit while that group sounds). The hatched strip on the right edge (the PO app's "more tools") opens **Live tools**: the view switch and **Follow** (switches to the group of the pad just played) on one card (with all four groups, **Mode**, PADS or KEYS, in Follow's place), the last KEYS note on a small display, and the notes on what is official folded under **How Live reads the EP-133**; in KEYS, the key as a one-octave piano, the colours as chips, and **How Keys works**. The page reads the sound names and the active project's pads once (as the device browser does), then listens; it changes nothing on the device unless you use **EDIT** or, on Android, **PROJECT** or SAMPLE's **KEEP**. arc keeps that read, so without the device (unplugged, or not yet connected) Live still shows the pads with their sample names as last seen, marked **Offline** with the time of the read (tap **Offline** for a note on why). Before any read, Live can play the EP-133's **factory sounds**: Settings → Live → **Factory sounds** → **Get** (also first in Live tools while nothing is connected) downloads the factory pack from teenage engineering's EP Sample Tool, the file their tool restores a device with (about 27 MB), into the library as a backup of its own that Keep never deletes; Live then shows its project 1, marked **Offline** · **Factory sounds**, until it has read your EP-133; and a pad whose sound the device lists unnamed ("343.pcm", as it lists the factory sounds) plays from the pack when arc has no copy of its own. This is arc's only use of the internet (Android has the permission for it alone; the web reads it through arc's host, which forwards only `/te/apps/ep-sample-tool/` to teenage engineering). Tap a pad, connected or not, to hear its sample on the phone. arc plays it from its own copy of the device's sounds: while Live is open with the device connected, it copies the samples on the active project's pads one at a time in the background, and any action you start waits at most for the sound being copied. Without a copy it plays from the newest backup holding that sound, or downloads it from the device when connected. Several pads or keys play at once (up to 8 sounds), so held fingers make a chord. A pad or key sounds only while it is held, as in the EP-133's gate mode: it starts when touched, each finger on its own, and fades out quickly when the finger lifts (the quickest tap still sounds briefly). On the scrolling all-groups page a press sounds at once too; if the finger goes on to scroll within a moment, the sound is cut short. A screen reader's Play plays the whole sample. A finger's press also gives a light haptic tick, after the sound has started (Settings → Live → **Haptics**, on by default; nothing on release). To keep the delay from press to sound short, Live keeps one low-latency sound output open while it is on screen, at the phone's own sample rate, and mixes the pads and keys into it itself, converting the EP-133's 46875 Hz and the KEYS pitch as it goes; the samples on the active project's pads are loaded into memory when Live opens and handed to the output in the background, so a press doesn't read, decode or copy anything. That output is a native engine: Google's Oboe library over AAudio, exclusive (MMAP) where the phone allows it, mixing in its own audio callback with a C++ port of the Kotlin mixer that renders the same samples. Where it can't start, or dies or stalls while playing, Live switches to the Kotlin output (an AudioTrack on Android's fast mixer path, each burst mixed just before it is needed) and stays on it until arc restarts. Either way the buffer starts at two bursts, grows after a dropout and shrinks back after a quiet while; the output is tagged as a game's sound (it still follows the media volume), and while Live is shown the touch screen hands over each move as it happens rather than once a frame. The debug log notes which output is in use and how it was set up and, for each press, how long after it the sound left the phone. Bluetooth adds its own delay (often 0.2 s or more): arc points it out once, and while the sound goes to Bluetooth or a hearing aid the display line says **Bluetooth plays late: wired or the speaker is quicker**. Each pad playing gets an orange ring. The word right under the grid (**PADS** with the PO app's two-squares mark, like its DRUMS / KEYPAD) switches to KEYS on a tap, and back, after the EP-133's KEYS mode and the PO app's melodic grid: the 12 pads become notes of one sound (the pad last tapped, or last played on the device), named in fixed-do solfège (DO DI RE … TI) or, by a Settings choice, letters (C C# D … B) (a second switch there, **Key labels**, turns the names off), the scale's root in orange and its other notes in the key's ink; a named key shows only its name, and with names off each grid key shows a ring in that colour instead (the piano never has rings: its root has an orange bar at its foot, and keys outside the scale are dimmed) (the number in its corner is the octave; a small legend in Live tools explains the colours). Tap a key to hear that sample repitched on the phone (like a sampler: higher is shorter), offline too; in KEYS the row under the keys also holds the scale and the octave as words in the same style (tap either for its list: chromatic, major, minor, the modes, pentatonics, blues; octaves 0 to 8), and Live tools picks the key. Notes the EP-133 sends in its own KEYS mode light their key, by name when its octave differs. Settings shows the space the copies take and clears them.
  - **Piano:** turn the phone on its side and KEYS becomes a piano with every semitone. It starts on the C an octave under OCT's C and shows as many keys as fit with each white key at least 44 dp wide: one octave on the narrowest phones, 1½ (DO3 to SO4 at OCT 4) or 2 on phones, 3 on a tablet. On the two-octave plate (a Pixel 7) it runs from DO3 to DO5 (C3–C5) at OCT 4, so the sample's own pitch is in the middle. The key's root has an orange bar at its foot (and, with names on, its name in orange), the scale's other notes are named in ink, and the other keys are dimmed and unnamed but still play; there are no rings. Slide a finger across the keys to run through the notes, and hold several for a chord. The row above the keys holds the mode, the scale, a **KEY** word that picks the root, and the octave between **−** and **+**, which shift it an octave as the EP-133's own − and + do. Notes from the EP-133 light their exact key; one past either end puts an orange mark at that end, and the display line names it when nothing is playing on the phone. Screen readers read each key's note and whether it is the root, in the scale or outside it, with Play to hear it. In PADS, one group fills the height with A–D beside it, and all four groups sit in one row (a narrow window keeps the scrolling page, so the pads stay at least 40 dp).
  - **Pads or piano:** two small keys right after the KEYS word (a grid and a piano) switch KEYS between the 3×4 grid and the piano wherever the piano fits, a tablet held upright included. arc remembers the choice separately for wide and tall windows. A phone held upright always uses the grid and doesn't show the switch; the piano key is greyed out where fewer than 8 white keys fit or the room is under 120 dp tall.
  - **Piano size:** Settings → Live → **Piano keys** picks Auto (as many as fit), 1 octave, 1½, 2 or 3 octaves. Sizes that don't fit the window are greyed out there, and if the window shrinks the piano falls back to the largest size that fits.
  - **EDIT: give a pad another sound.** In PADS, **EDIT** (on Android the **SOUND** function key, its lower half printed EDIT; on the web an **EDIT** tab under GUIDE on the left edge, on the desktop layout on the K.O. II panel's left side) turns the display line orange and marks every pad. Tap a pad for its sheet: the pad, its project and the sound on it now, and (on Android) the pad's **sound settings** as the EP-133's SOUND EDIT pages have them: a row of knobs, **PITCH** and **LEVEL** always there, then the X and Y knobs of the page picked under them, **SOUND** (play mode ONESHOT / KEY / LEGATO, pan), **TRIM** (start and length over the sound's waveform), **ENV** (attack, release), **MIDI** (channel) and **MUTE** (mute group). A drag turns a knob (slowly for fine steps), a double tap resets it. The pad plays with them on the phone at once, and they go on the EP-133 once the turning rests, the whole pad record each time (a part could make the device take the rest from the sample again); offline they are kept in arc and offered with the other offline changes, and then only what was turned is written, over what the device has, on a pad still holding the same sound. TIME and the root note are left alone: they belong to the sample, for every pad it is on. Below them, under **Change sound** (straight away on the web and on an empty pad): a search field, keys for each range of slots, and the device's sounds with a Play key each (the one on the pad marked ON PAD). Tap a sound and the pad takes it at once, through the same project file write the EP-133 makes; the pad's own settings (level, pitch and the rest) start again from the sample's, as when you change a pad's sound on the device. A message names the new sound with **UNDO**, offered when the old sound is known. **Upload a new sample…** puts a WAV in the first free slot and on the pad. Held past a long press, a pad still plays in EDIT. On the web's desktop layout, the tools column has **Tools** and **Sounds** tabs: drag a sound from Sounds onto a pad, or drop a WAV on a pad to upload it and put it there; right-click a pad for its sheet. **Not yet tried on a real EP-133**: see [Status](#status-what-is-verified-and-what-still-needs-a-real-device).
  - **EDIT offline:** with the device unplugged, EDIT works on Live's last read (or the factory sounds) and changes the pads in arc only. The sheet has a **Sounds from Device / Factory** switch: the device's sounds as last read, without sizes, or the saved factory pack's. Device sounds arc has no copy or backup of are dimmed and marked **Needs the EP-133** (the pad's own sound in the read stays pickable, to take a change back); the rest preview on the phone. A sound put on a pad names it and plays from arc's copy, a backup or, for a factory sound, the pack. The changes survive a restart (in the app's own files as `live-pads.json`, not in Documents/arc) and **Live tools → Offline pad changes → Reset pads** drops them. When the EP-133 connects and Live has read it, arc asks **Put N offline pad changes on the EP-133?**: **Write** puts on each change made on the device's active project whose sound the device still holds in that slot, skips the others and counts both in a message; **Discard** leaves the device as it is. Either way the changes are cleared.
  - **Function keys (Android):** four keys printed as the EP-133's two-tier keys, SOUND · PROJECT · TEMPO · FX, each under an LED and a label: a row over the pads or KEYS' grid (upright, on the all-groups and tablet pages, over a tablet's piano), a column left of them on a phone on its side (it shrinks to fit a short window, dropping its LED lines last). **SOUND** is EDIT's key (above): a tap turns EDIT on or off (from KEYS it goes back to the pads), its LED lit while EDIT is on, and a hold opens the sheet of the pad played last; it takes the place of the EDIT edge tab. **PROJECT** steps to the next project, 1 to 9 and round again (held, a tap on a pad printed 1 to 9 goes straight to that project, as on the EP-133, while the other pads stay still; let go without one and a long hold opens the **Project** sheet, 1 to 9 in the keypad's order; offline only the projects arc can show): connected, the EP-133 switches project and Live follows it once read (taps while it switches pile up, the newest is written; a failure says why); offline with the factory pack, Live steps between the last read's project and the pack's, in arc only; with neither it is greyed out. **KEYS / PADS** is printed down the right margin of the pads' plate (one group, and KEYS' grid; on its side too), the mode shown in ink and the other grey: the whole strip is one switch, a tap goes to the other mode. The piano has the mode word at the start of the row over it instead, and on the all-groups page the mode is in Live tools, under the view switch. **TEMPO** is a click on the phone: a tap turns it on or off, a hold opens the **Tempo** sheet (−/+, tap tempo, 40–240 BPM). The click has its own small output, so TAKE leaves it out, accents each bar's first beat, and shares Live's audio focus; while the EP-133 sends MIDI clock it follows the device's tempo, and after a Start its beat and bar; while a pattern plays it clicks on the pattern's bars; and the LED blinks on each beat. **FX** (its lower half printed PAGE) opens the **FX** sheet, its LED lit under the effect's name while one is on (FX OFF for none): **EFFECT** has the six effects (DLY, REV, DST, CHO, FLT, CMP; the one on, tapped again, turns off), an XY pad on the display for the effect's X and Y (the dot follows the finger, what each does reads in its corner) and a send fader per group, A–D (the pad played last's group in orange); **OUTPUT** has the output compressor (on or off, DRIVE, SPEED) and the sidechain (on or off; SOURCE, set to the pad played last, ducks the groups picked under DUCKS; LENGTH and SHAPE of the duck). Each project keeps its own FX in arc. **FX held** turns the one-group pads into the twelve punch-ins, each named where the pad shows its sound, by the label printed on it as on the EP-133 (7 TREMOLO, 8 OCT ↓, 9 DECIMATE, 4 LPF, 5 HPF, 6 SEND FX, 1 REPEAT, 2 TAPE STOP, 3 FILTER LFO, • PITCH RND, 0 SLICE, ENTER STUTTER): a pad held punches its effect in, several together combine, and FX's upper half and the held pads light orange, a bar at each held pad's foot showing how deep it goes. The depth is the touch's pressure on a screen that reports one that varies, else how high up the pad the finger is (the top all the way, the foot lightest); a finger moving changes it. The display line names those held (**PUNCH · REPEAT + LPF**); letting go of FX lets go of them all and gives the pads back. Punch-ins start no voice and go into no pattern; a screen reader's long click on FX turns them on and off, and a click on a pad puts its punch-in in until clicked again. The web has the PADS word and no function keys yet.
  - **SAMPLE: record into a pad (Android).** Tap the mic key in the top bar (round, before **?** and the gear, on the Live tab only; tagged in the **?** guide): the dark display line itself grows into the **SAMPLE** panel over the function keys' place (their row upright, their column on its side): its words fade out and SAMPLE's header fades in in their place (never the two over each other), its rounded dark shape grows down to hold a wave strip, and a pale plate with the controls unrolls under it, one rounded body like the device's screen over its keys; the wave fades in and then the two rows of controls, while the function keys fade and shrink away and the pads glide down to make room (on its side they shrink beside it); the pads stay on the page and record. It opens in about 300 ms, and the mic key turns orange while it is open; another tap closes it. In KEYS, the mic key goes to PADS and opens the panel in the same tap. On a phone narrower than 400 dp, and on a small phone on its side with the display line in the top bar, the top bar's keys close up in their two groups (the backup squares, then the round keys) to make room for it; beside the display line they also come down a size (40 dp) and close up to it, so the line keeps the pad's sound name as it did before the mic key. On the all-groups page scrolled down to the lower groups, the page goes back up as it opens, so the panel unrolls in sight. Open, nothing hangs under the panel: the pads sit right under it, at the page's usual gap, and closed there is nothing between the function keys and the pads. The mic key closes it again, a swipe on the pads from right to left opens it too and one back closes it, as does Back; opening and closing give a light tick (Haptics), and closing takes about 240 ms. Opening it stops TEMPO's click, whose key it covers, so the phone's speaker doesn't play into a MIC take (BARS' count-in still clicks its bar). With Android's animations off the panel opens and closes at once. The swipe starts anywhere on the pads: a pad under the finger sounds at once and is cut short once the move is a swipe, and a move more up than across still scrolls the all-groups page. The panel is there in PADS on every layout (one group, all groups, on its side, a tablet), not in KEYS. While it is open SAMPLE mode is on, and the line's own row, still dark, is SAMPLE's header and the only status on the page (there is no separate orange line): an orange light, the source, a meter and what happens next (**Hold a pad to record**, **Waiting for sound**, **Count-in 2**, **0:04 / 0:20**, **Pad A 7: uploading, 40%**). TalkBack hears it as the SAMPLE pane, and reads what happens next as it changes. Upright, where the pads under it would come out narrower than a finger, the panel gives up its wave strip first (the header still says it all), then shrinks its knobs to a key's height, then lays its controls in one row; on its side (the line on the page, in a narrow window) the line first narrows to the panel's width while the pads come aside under it, then grows down the left of the column into the panel as they rise beside it, so neither is ever over the other, the wave strip taking all the column's height the controls (and the USB note) leave (none where that is short of a strip), so the plate holds just its controls, and the controls scrolling in a short window. On a phone on its side with the display line in the top bar, the line can't grow into the column: there it fades through to SAMPLE's header in the bar, in step with the panel (and back as it closes), and the panel under it, without a header of its own, holds the wave strip (as tall as the column lets it be) and the controls. The status is always one line, in smaller type where the header is narrow; a narrow header (on its side, a 360 dp phone) leaves the source to the source window on the plate right under it. In a short window on its side the knobs' names and values come down a size, so none is cut off, and on a tablet the controls keep together in the middle of the plate, as wide as a phone's. The page's pads are the ones to record into, in every layout, and A–D still pick the group. The header shows the source, a 16-segment meter from −60 to 0 dBFS with the threshold's orange mark and a clip light, and what happens next (the take's time against the longest it can be while it records); the wave strip under it shows the input's level going by while a take waits or records, else the last take's waveform, or before the first take how long one can be (**Takes up to 40 s**). The controls follow the EP-133's sampler:
    - **−** and **+** step the source in the device's order: MIC, MIC ST, RSP, RSP ST, USB, USB ST. **STEREO** switches mono and stereo (greyed where the source has only one).
    - **LEVEL** (knob X) is the input's gain, −12 to +30 dB, kept for each source (the mic starts at +12). **THRESH** (knob Y) is Off at its left end, so a take starts at the press; from −60 to 0 dB a take waits for the first sound that loud and keeps the 20 ms before it, so its attack isn't cut.
    - **BARS** (Free, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, and **PTN** while the project has pattern notes) sets a hands-free take's length, and **LATCH** turns on hands-free takes.
    - **MIC** is the phone's mic, as raw as the phone gives it (Android's UNPROCESSED source where the phone has it, else VOICE_RECOGNITION, which skips noise suppression and gain control, else the plain mic). **RSP** resamples Live's own mix, everything the phone plays but the click, so pads played while a take records go into it, chords included (a playing pattern too), and TAKE can record beside it. **USB** (experimental) records the EP-133's own sound over USB audio, which needs OS 2.5 and its sound going out over USB; it is offered only while the EP-133's USB input is there, and if it goes away the recording so far is kept and RSP stands in.

    Hold a pad to record into it: the take starts at the touch (or at the threshold) and stops when the finger lifts. A quick tap records nothing: it plays a pad that has a sound, and on an empty one a message says to hold it. As on the device, empty pads blink, pads with a sound stay lit and the pad recording lights up; other pads play as usual meanwhile. With **LATCH** on, a tap starts a hands-free take (for one hand, or a screen reader): LATCH then reads **STOP**, and STOP or a tap on that pad stops it. With BARS set, a hands-free take lasts that many bars of the tempo after a one-bar count-in on the click (the click comes on for it, and the display counts the beats); from USB while the EP-133 sends MIDI clock it waits for the device's **PLAY** instead and lasts that many bars of its tempo. While a pattern plays (below), a BARS take starts on its next bar, with no count-in. **PTN** resamples the pattern: the take lasts as long as the longest group's pattern and starts at its start, the patterns starting for it from bar 1 when stopped (playing, not recording), or at the next loop when they play. RSP's take starts at that exact frame of Live's mix, so the take is the loop to the sample; MIC and USB start as the bar is heard. A take is at most 20 s in stereo or 40 s in mono, as on OS 2.5, and stops by itself there; when the EP-133's free space (as last read) can't hold a full take, the display says **Disk low: room for 12 s** and takes stop sooner. With Haptics on, each count-in beat ticks, and a take starting, or stopping by itself, gives a long buzz.

    Each take opens the **New sample** sheet: the waveform with the part kept lit, **START** and **LENGTH** knobs, a play key for that part as it will go on the pad, **Normalize** (its loudest point to 0 dB) and **Trim silence** (it starts where the sound does), both kept for the next take, and, connected, the slot it goes into (the next free one; − and + step over the free slots). Then **KEEP**, **RETAKE** (a hands-free take records again at once, a held one at the next hold) or **DISCARD**, with **UNDO**; closing the sheet discards too. Settings → Live → **Review samples** off skips the sheet and puts each take straight on its pad, as the EP-133 does. KEEP puts the take on its pad at once: the pad plays it from the phone with fresh settings, as on the device after a new sample. Connected, it uploads in the background into its slot, named after its source and time (`mic 1007-142301`), while Live plays on; SAMPLE's header shows the progress and a message says when it is on the EP-133. If the upload fails, the pad keeps playing it from arc and arc asks straight away whether to write it again. Offline it waits in arc's own files (not Documents/arc) until the EP-133 connects, and the offline changes question counts it (**Put 1 new sample on the EP-133?**): **Write** uploads it into the next free slot and onto its pad. Nothing recorded is lost. Each of these goes to **Live tools → Takes**: a take with no pad to go on (no project read yet), a recording that Discard, Reset pads or a newer take replaces before it is on the device, one that Write can't put on, and one arc was closed on before KEEP. Closing the panel stops a take going on and keeps it, whether by the mic key, a swipe, Back, KEYS, EDIT, another tab or arc leaving the screen (back in arc, a message says so). The first open with MIC or USB asks for the microphone. Refused, RSP stands in (it needs no permission), and once Android stops asking, a message says so with a key to arc's settings. Android's mic indicator shows while the panel is open on MIC or USB. Screen readers hear the source, the line as it changes, and each pad's state (empty, has a sound, recording, waiting to record). The mic key is a button, **Sample**, saying **On** or **Off**, and the panel is announced by its title. A pad's click latches a hands-free take, stops it or cancels it, and its Play action plays a pad with a sound. The web has no SAMPLE yet: its core has the sampling logic and the text, with the same tests, but no input or screens.
  - **Patterns: RECORD and PLAY (Android).** As on the EP-133, the pads played go into a looping pattern that plays back. Live's display line starts with a **● RECORD** and a **▶ PLAY** chip (glyphs alone in the top bar's line on a phone on its side; a row of their own on the all-groups display). Tap RECORD to arm it (its light blinks and the line says **Play a pad or PLAY · 1/16**), then PLAY: a bar's count-in on the click, four beats with the first accented, shown as a big digit (**3 / 4**) with a haptic tick each (Haptics), then recording from bar 1. RECORD and PLAY together start at once, as on the device, and **COUNT-IN** off in the pattern sheet skips it always. Armed, the first pad (or KEYS note) played starts recording right there, without PLAY, as on the device: bar 1 begins on that press, which is the pattern's first note. While it plays, a tap on RECORD punches recording in or out, and RECORD held records only while it is held (a punch-in for as long as the finger stays); PLAY, a ■ meanwhile, stops. Each pass overdubs: a note on the same pad and pitch at the same step replaces the old one. Notes snap to **TIMING** (Off, 1/8, 1/16 as on the device, 1/32); a note the grid moves later than you heard it isn't played again in that pass, so it doesn't flam. Each note keeps how long it was held, from the touch to the lift, and KEYS notes keep their pitch too, on the KEYS sound's pad. While it runs the line shows where the group played last is (**2.3 / 4**, with the grid while recording, **2.3 / 4 · 1/16**), a 2 dp hairline along its foot going round the loop, signal with a signal border while recording; the EP-133's own ▶/■ steps aside meanwhile. The pads ring and the KEYS keys outline as the pattern plays them.
    - **The pattern sheet:** hold RECORD (stopped or armed) for it. **LENGTH** for each group, A–D: 1 to 99 bars with − and + (held, they repeat), 1, 2, 4 and 8 at a tap, and **×2**, which doubles a group and copies its notes into the new half (SHIFT + + on the device). Notes past a shortened end are kept, not played. Each group loops at its own length, so a 1-bar beat runs under a 4-bar bass line. **AUTO** length: an empty group recorded from stop ends where recording stops, at 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars. TIMING, COUNT-IN, **UNDO**, ERASE and **CLEAR** (a group's notes or every group's, asked in the sheet first; the lengths stay). Pads the pattern plays whose sounds aren't on the phone yet are counted there (**3 pads not loaded**): they load quietly from arc's copies or a backup and play once they have.
    - **ERASE** latches, from the sheet or a chip on the line (while the project has notes and the pattern plays, where it fits; on a tablet's wide line while stopped too): pads with notes get a small signal dot, a tap erases a pad's notes (a message says so), and a pad held while the pattern plays erases its notes as the playhead passes, as ERASE + pad does on the device; in KEYS a key erases that note the same way. ERASE and EDIT turn each other off, and leaving Live turns ERASE off.
    - **↶ UNDO** (SHIFT + B on the device), in the sheet and beside ERASE on the line: back to before the last pass recorded, erase, clear or length change, up to 32 steps for each project while arc runs.
    - **Playing:** on the phone, online and offline, whatever sound is on each pad, as the device's patterns do. The notes go into Live's mix to the frame, scheduled 50 ms ahead on the output's own thread, so a busy screen doesn't make them late. The tempo is TEMPO's (the EP-133's while it sends MIDI clock, else the phone's), and changing it mid-play keeps the pattern in time. A call stops it, as does leaving Live or arc; recording stops where it was heard, and the patterns are kept. Opening SAMPLE stops recording but PLAY goes on, for PTN and BARS takes in step with it.
    - **Saved per project** in arc's own files (`patterns.json`, not Documents/arc, so an uninstall loses them), connected or not; TIMING, COUNT-IN, AUTO and SAMPLE's PTN go with the settings in `library.json`. Nothing is written to the EP-133.
    - Screen readers hear RECORD and PLAY as buttons with their state (**Record, armed**, **Play, bar 2 of 4**) and a hold on RECORD as **Pattern settings**, and the transport's changes once each (**Counting in**, **Recording**, **Stopped**). The web has the pattern's logic and text in its core, with the same tests, but no RECORD or PLAY yet.
  - Pads light up in the keypad layout as notes arrive, brighter with velocity, and fade on release. This follows the official MIDI note map: notes 36–83, one octave per group.
  - Play/stop and tempo come from MIDI clock, which the device sends only with clock out switched on (SHIFT + ERASE, then 102 and ENTER).
  - Notes outside the pads, from KEYS mode, show on a keyboard strip with their channel.
  - **Sample names on the pads** rely on community notes, not the official guide:
    - A physical pad press also makes the device send its pad file id over SysEx.
    - arc pairs that with the note to learn which pad is which, then names the sample from the project's pads. One press of a key, in any group, names that key in every group, and arc remembers it; nothing is guessed, and two pads of one group hit together don't count.
    - Community notes disagree on how project files number the pads, so Settings → Live → **Pad numbers** counts them from the top or from the bottom.
- **Record what you play (TAKE):** **Live tools → Takes** starts with a **● TAKE** key that records the pads and keys played on the phone into a take, with or without the EP-133 (it was REC, on the display line, before RECORD became the pattern's, as on the device). Tap it to arm: recording starts with the first sound, so a take has no silence in front, and the key counts the time. While a take is armed or records, a small **● TAKE 0:12** badge sits on Live's display line (in the top bar, with the line, on a phone on its side); a tap on it, or on the key, stops it. Silence after the last sound is left out, and if nothing was played nothing is saved. Leaving Live stops and saves a take too, and one stops by itself at 10 minutes. A take is a stereo 16-bit WAV at the phone's output rate (usually 48 kHz), copied from Live's own mix as it is played, so it sounds exactly as you heard it (gates, chords, KEYS pitch and a playing pattern included) but holds nothing the EP-133 itself plays. The Takes list, newest first: play one, or tap it to share or save the WAV, delete it, or, while the EP-133 is connected, send it **To EP-133**, which opens the Device tab's upload sheet with the take (pick the slot, trim it, upload; above 46875 Hz it is resampled as any upload is). Takes stay in arc's own storage until deleted. SAMPLE's recordings that have no pad to go on land there too (above), and its RSP can record while TAKE does.
- **Settings:** the gear at the top right (on the web's desktop layout, the Settings key at the foot of the left column; the top bar has a System / Light / Dark switch there instead). Theme (system, light or dark); whether arc connects by itself when an EP-133 is plugged in; whether the screen stays on in Live; how many backups to keep (all, 5, 10 or 20: after each backup or import the oldest beyond that are deleted, here and in Documents/arc, and lowering it asks first); Live's pad numbering, note names, key labels, the piano's size, haptics and whether SAMPLE's takes open the review sheet; forgetting learned sample names and clearing the pad sound copies (outlined in red, as they delete); the version, source, font licence and the debug log. Each row shows just its name and its control; the ⓘ after the name unfolds its note (and the longer one, where there is more to say), and switches are keys with a light. A wide window (720 dp and up on Android, 1024px on the web) adds a list of the sections on the left, its light following the section in view. The settings are saved in Documents/arc with the library, so they come back after a reinstall.
- **Shortcut guide:** 100 EP-133 key combinations in tabs by section (each with its count, the matches while you search), with search. Each row is what it does, then the combination as small keys in the device's colours with SOUND MODE, HOLD, TYPE, TURN and MOVE tags; open a row for numbered steps (1 hold SOUND → 2 type a number on the pads), the note and the source. A wide window (840 dp and up, not short; 1024px on the web) draws the K.O. II beside the list: the open row's keys are outlined orange with their step numbers, and the rest of the panel dims. A test checks that every key drawn is named in that entry's text from the official guide. Every entry is paraphrased from teenage engineering's official user guide for OS 2.5, and links to the section it comes from. Combos the guide doesn't document are left out.

Backups use the same layout as the official Sample Tool's `.pak`: a zip with `/meta.json`, `/sounds/NNN name.wav` and `/projects/PNN.tar`. On top of that, an `arc.json` file keeps per-sound settings like play mode, pitch and envelope.

A debug screen (Settings → **Debug log**, or long-press the section tag) shows every SysEx message sent and received, and can share, save or copy the log as a text file. Its **Latency** test compares Live's audio engines on your own phone or browser (see [Measuring latency](#measuring-latency)).

## Web app

The same app runs in Chrome or Edge, on a computer or an Android phone, at **https://arc-pi-mauve.vercel.app**. It talks to the EP-133 over USB-C with WebMIDI. Everything above is there: backup, restore, the library, contents, compare, search, the device browser, sample upload with trim, pads, the live mirror (opening on Live, shown as one group with A–D by default, pads that sound while held, chords of up to 8 sounds, KEYS with solfège or letter note names and, wherever it fits, the piano, and Offline from the last read, where EDIT and the desktop's **Sounds** tab change pads in arc only until the EP-133 connects), settings, the shortcut guide and the debug log. It looks and reads like the Android app, and its `.pak` files and `library.json` are the same files the Android app writes, so a library moves between the two.

The browser asks once for MIDI access when you connect. It can be installed from the install icon in the address bar or the browser's menu (**Add to Home screen** on Android), and then opens in its own window like an app. After the first visit it works offline. A new version is downloaded in the background and shows **Reload**; arc never reloads in the middle of a transfer.

On a computer the keyboard plays and drives Live: the number pad (or the number row) plays the pads as the EP-133's keypad, A–D pick the group, E is EDIT, M switches pads and keys, and in KEYS the letters play the piano, Z X the octave, [ ] the key and Shift+[ ] the scale. **?** lists the keys that work where you are; Esc closes a screen. Settings → Live → **Computer keyboard** turns the single keys off.

| Browser | EP-133 over USB |
|---|---|
| Chrome, Edge, Opera, Brave on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS | Yes |
| Chrome on Android | Yes |
| Firefox on a computer | Only after you accept Firefox's prompt to install its site permission add-on for MIDI |
| Safari, and every browser on iPhone and iPad | No. The library still works: import, contents, playback, export, compare, search and the guide |

Where WebMIDI is missing, arc says so on the panel and the controls that need the device are disabled; the rest works as usual. The page needs HTTPS (or `localhost` during development) for MIDI.

How it differs from the Android app:
- **No background transfers.** A browser has no foreground service. Keep the tab in front and the cable plugged in until a backup or restore is done: in a background tab timers slow down and the transfer can stall. While a task runs the screen is kept on, the tab title shows the progress, leaving the page asks first, and hiding the tab shows a reminder. Cancel is on the progress sheet.
- **The library lives in the browser** (IndexedDB), so clearing the site's data deletes it. arc asks the browser to keep it.
  - On Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers on a computer, **Pick a library folder** keeps a copy of every backup outside the browser, the same `.pak` files and `library.json` as Documents/arc on Android. Pick a folder named `arc`, or one that already holds arc backups. After a browser restart, one tap on **Reconnect library folder** gives arc access again.
  - Elsewhere, including Chrome on Android, **Export library** saves every backup and `library.json` as one zip (`arc-library.zip`); export now and then to keep a copy.
  - **Restore from a folder** also works without a library folder: pick an arc folder (one written by this app, Documents/arc copied from a phone, or an unzipped export) and its backups, titles, notes and settings come in. Backups already in the library are skipped.
- **Opening a `.pak`:** double-clicking a `.pak` opens it in arc only with the installed app on a computer with a Chromium browser. Everywhere else, use **Import** or drag the file onto the page.
- **Sharing** uses the browser's share sheet where it can take files; otherwise the file is saved instead, and arc says so.
- **The piano** in KEYS shows wherever it fits, as on Android (the pads-or-piano switch after the KEYS word is remembered for wide and tall windows): the same range, dimmed keys, root bar, slides and chords, − and + for the octave, and the KEY word, whose list Back closes. A mouse plays one note at a time; with a keyboard, Tab reaches one key, the arrow keys, Home and End move along them, and Enter or Space plays it (the letter keys play it too, below). On a phone on its side (a window under 480 px high and at least 600 wide, not the desktop layout) the display line moves into the top bar and the messages show there too, two lines at most, as on Android.

The output is set up as soon as Live opens, before any tap, so the first press only has to wake it; leaving Live or hiding the tab pauses it rather than closing it (it is let go of after a minute away, or when the page closes), so coming back plays at once. A browser only starts sound after a tap, so on a phone the very first touch on a fresh page may sound only briefly; the next ones play normally.

### Get a test build

Every pull request and every push to `main` runs [`.github/workflows/android.yml`](.github/workflows/android.yml). It installs the NDK and CMake, runs the tests (the native engine's host test included), then lint, then builds the debug APK.

1. Open the latest run under the repository's **Actions** tab.
2. Download the `arc-debug-apk` artifact and unzip it.
3. Install `app-debug.apk`, either with `adb install -r app-debug.apk` or by opening the file on the phone and allowing installs from that source.

### Versions and releases

arc follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/). A major version breaks `.pak` or library compatibility, a minor version adds features, a patch version fixes bugs. Changes are listed in [`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md).

- **One place:** `version.properties` (`version=0.3.5`) sets the version. The build fails if it isn't `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH` with minor and patch below 100.
- **versionCode** is computed from it: `major*10000 + minor*100 + patch` (0.3.5 is 305), so every new version installs over the previous one.
- **versionName:** local builds are `0.3.5-dev`; CI builds add the run number and commit, `0.3.5-dev.57+abc1234`; release builds are exactly `0.3.5`. It shows in **Settings** and at the top of the debug log.
- **.pak files** name the version that wrote them in `meta.json` (`"author": "arc 0.3.5"`, without the `-dev` part). The compatibility test builds its backup with the web version's own version string so it can still compare byte for byte.
- **To release:**
  1. Bump `version.properties` and move the `Unreleased` notes in `CHANGELOG.md` under `## [X.Y.Z] - date`. Commit and merge.
  2. `git tag vX.Y.Z && git push origin vX.Y.Z`.
  3. The **Release** workflow checks that the tag matches `version.properties` and that the changelog has that section. It then runs the tests, lint and build, and publishes `arc-X.Y.Z.apk` as a GitHub Release with those notes.

  The release APK is signed with the same shared debug key as every CI build, so it installs over test builds. There is no separate release key yet.

## Faithful to the web version

The EP-133 file protocol is undocumented and was reverse engineered, so the JS is ported line by line. These behaviours are kept on purpose:

- Every request carries a 12-bit request id in bytes 6 and 7, and replies are matched by that id.
- After every completed file download or upload, the app redoes the handshake (identity, greet, file init). Otherwise the device drops the next command.
- Download page numbers are 14-bit little-endian, and the echo check accepts either byte order. One empty data page is tolerated; two in a row are an error.
- Uploads:
  - Data goes out in 433-byte chunks with a window of 16 unacknowledged frames.
  - If no ack ever arrives, the app stops windowing after one 600 ms stall and streams the rest.
  - A barrier request after the last chunk confirms the device has processed everything.
- Metadata writes are capped at 320 bytes. Optional sound keys are dropped in the web version's order to fit.
- Sound uploads are verified by comparing the device's crc with a CRC32 of the PCM, with one retry.
- After a project upload, the app switches the active project away and back. The originally active project is restored at the end.
- Restore checks free space before writing anything.
- Sample rates above 46875 Hz are resampled down, and loop points are scaled to match.
- If the device lists no projects, the backup probes projects 1 to 9.

The behaviours above are commented where they happen in the code. The same goes for odd details of the JS that were kept, such as how "Also restore sounds no picked project uses" really means sounds that no project in the backup uses.

## Deliberate differences

- **Three web-only sentences are reworded for a phone:**
  - "No MIDI on this phone"
  - "This phone can't connect to your EP-133. Your saved backups still work here."
  - "MIDI access was blocked. Unplug the EP-133, plug it back in, then connect again."

  The "this browser can't share files" fallback is gone, because Android always has a share sheet.
- **Auto-connect:** arc connects by itself when an EP-133 is plugged in while the app is open and idle.
- **Hangs become errors:** two inputs that make the JS hang now fail with an error instead. These are a crafted `.pak` whose tar has a negative size, and a WAV with 0 channels.
- **No stale ack handlers:** when an upload fails, its pending ack handlers are dropped, so they can never catch a later reply after the request id wraps.
- **Different error wording for damaged zip data:** the browser's own wording can't be reproduced.
- **Live's function keys are Android only:** SOUND, PROJECT, TEMPO (the phone's click and the tempo sheet) and FX (the FX sheet) and the KEYS / PADS strip on the pads' plate have no web equivalent yet; the web keeps the PADS / KEYS word and its EDIT tab. PROJECT switches the device's active project with the same metadata write a restore uses. The click is a separate output, never part of TAKE's takes.
- **A pad's sound settings are Android only:** EDIT's pages (pitch, level, pan, play mode, trim, envelope, MIDI channel, mute group) and the phone playing pads with them have no web screens yet; the web's pad sheet changes a pad's sound only. The model, the device read and write and the mixer's voice shape are ported to the web's core already, unused there. They write a pad's metadata the way EDIT's sound change does.
- **SAMPLE is Android only:** the SAMPLE panel, its inputs (the phone's mic, Live's mix, the EP-133 over USB audio), the review sheet and the uploads have no web equivalent yet: the web's Live has no SAMPLE panel. Its pure logic is ported to the web's core already, unused there: the take's capture with the threshold and its pre-roll, the meter, the limits and Disk low, the review's edits, the count-in and PLAY timing, recordings as offline pad changes and uploads into a given slot, with the text. Recording into a pad is an addition: the EP-133 samples on the device itself, and arc uses only commands it already uses (KEEP's upload is EDIT's **Upload a new sample…** onto the pad).
- **Patterns stay in arc:** RECORD and PLAY record and play on the phone only. Patterns are never written to the EP-133 or read from it, and no MIDI goes to it; they play whatever sound is on each pad, as Live's pads do. Next to the device's sequencer:
  - one pattern per group, for each project; no scenes, no FX, no swing, no step sequencing (notes go in only as played) and no note repeat;
  - PLAY always starts at bar 1, never where the pattern stopped;
  - arc's own clock: the tempo follows the EP-133's while it sends MIDI clock, but the phase doesn't, so bar 1 is wherever PLAY (or, armed, the first pad) was pressed on the phone, not the device's;
  - TIMING is Off, 1/8, 1/16 or 1/32; AUTO length is an addition (1, 2, 4 or 8 bars), and so are SAMPLE's PTN and its bar-locked starts;
  - Android only: the web's core has the logic and the text, with the same tests, but no screens or sequencer yet.
- **New features:** the device browser, sample upload, compare, contents, trim, pads, search and guide screens have no web equivalent, so their wording is new. They use only commands the web version already uses (LIST, metadata get, file download, and the restore path for uploads). Playback, trimming and export happen on the phone.

### Measuring latency

How quickly Live sounds can only be measured on a real phone or browser, so the debug screen (Settings → **Debug log**, or long-press the section tag) has a **Latency** test for comparing before and after. Fold it out above the log, pick an **Audio engine**, open Live, tap one pad 20 times, come back and compare. Each engine tried keeps a row with the median, best and worst of its last 20 presses and an estimate from what its output reports (Android: its buffer and burst in frames over its rate; web: `baseLatency` + `outputLatency`). Only presses whose sample was already in memory count (one that had to load it is still in the debug log); every engine opened this session keeps its row, and **Reset** clears the times. A press is timed from the touch event's own timestamp (Android: the pointer event's uptime, moved onto Live's clock; web: the event's `timeStamp`), so the times include the input's way to the app, up to the first sound leaving the output; Bluetooth adds its own delay on top. The debug log's "heard … after the press" lines count the same way.

The engines: on Android, **Auto** (the native Oboe engine, AudioTrack where it won't open, as normally used), **AudioTrack** (each burst mixed just before it is due) and **AudioTrack, old** (mix, then a blocking write, no pacing, tagged as media, as before the latency work; touch input stays unbuffered as now, so it compares the output only); on the web, `latencyHint` **0** (now) and **'interactive'** (before; each row's estimate shows the base and output delay the browser reports, so a browser that quietly refuses 0 shows the same numbers on both). Changing it while Live is open reopens Live's output. The choice is a debug option kept on that phone or browser only, never in `library.json`, and starts at Auto or 0. On Android it has only been built and unit-tested so far.

17f. Patterns, connected and offline, with the debug log at hand:
   1. Tap **● RECORD** (it should blink), then **▶ PLAY**: the click should count 4 beats, the first accented, the line counting them big with a tick each, and recording should start on bar 1. Play a beat on group A: it should come back in time on the next pass. Try it on the speaker, wired headphones and Bluetooth (late on Bluetooth, but in time with itself).
   2. Overdub a second pass, then **↶**: only the last pass should go. Hold RECORD and PLAY together from stop: no count-in.
   2a. Armed, the first pad starts recording on bar 1: tap **● RECORD**, then play a beat on group A without PLAY. The line should go straight to **1.1**, recording, the first hit sounding once, and the beat should come back in time on the next pass, its first hit on bar 1.
   3. While playing, tap RECORD to punch in and again to punch out; then hold RECORD for a bar and let go: only that bar should be recorded.
   4. In KEYS, record a short melody with long and short notes: they should play back at their pitch and for as long as each was held.
   5. Record the same few hits with TIMING at Off, 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32: Off should keep them where played, each grid snap them to it.
   6. **ERASE**: tap a pad with notes (they should go, with a message); hold a pad while the pattern plays: its notes should go as the playhead passes, and only those.
   7. Set groups A–D to 1, 2, 4 and 8 bars and record into each: each should loop at its own length; **×2** on A should double it with its notes copied.
   8. Change the tempo mid-play, by TEMPO's sheet and with the EP-133's clock: the pattern should stay in time, and the click on the pattern's bars.
   9. Mid-play, plug headphones in and out, and switch the debug screen's engine: the pattern should come back in time after a short gap. Take a call: it should stop.
   10. Leave Live, and leave arc, mid-recording: the transport should stop and what was recorded be kept. Restart arc: each project's patterns should be back, connected and offline.
   11. SAMPLE on **RSP**, BARS on **PTN**, LATCH on: from stop, tap a pad: the patterns should start and the take last exactly their length; again while they play: from the next loop. Then a **● TAKE** while the pattern plays: it should hold the pattern.
   12. Record a dense pattern in all four groups and play pads over it: let go of everything and stop: no note should keep sounding. Run the debug screen's **Latency** test afterwards: the times should be as before.
   13. Resample a 1/16 hi-hat pattern with RSP and PTN, share the take and open it in a DAW at the same tempo: every onset should be within ±1 ms of the 1/16 grid.
18. Play a pattern: check whether sequenced pads light up. Switch on clock out (SHIFT + ERASE, 102, ENTER) and check play/stop and the tempo. Try KEYS mode.
19. Switch sections from the tag with a backup running and with Live open: the progress sheet should stay up, Live should stop listening when you leave it, arc should open on Live, and Back on any other section should return to Live. Open the guide from its tab on the left edge and close it with Back. Open **?** on each tab: every tag should point at its control without covering another tag (on a phone on its side, nor another control or another tag's arrow). Long-press each icon for its name, and check them with TalkBack.
20. Play a sound from a backup's **Contents** through the phone speaker, wired headphones and Bluetooth. If one stays silent, export the debug log: it has a "play … -> output" line naming where Android sent the sound.
21. In **Settings**: switch the theme; turn **Auto-connect** off and plug the EP-133 in (it should stay disconnected until you tap Connect); with 6 backups, set **Keep** to 5 and confirm (the oldest goes from the list and from Documents/arc); make a backup and check the oldest is removed again.
22. Turn the phone in Live's KEYS while holding a chord: the notes should fade out without a click, and the debug log should show no new read of the device. On its side, slide across the piano while holding a chord with the other hand (the chord should keep sounding), and try **−** and **+**. With the three-button bar on either side, and with gesture navigation, check that the strip on the right edge, its panel's ✕ and the GUIDE tab can be tapped, and that holding the keys at the edges doesn't go Back. Turn auto-rotate off: the rotate button Android shows should turn arc. On its side, type in the Device tab's find field, in **Search** and in a backup's sheet. Check the piano's keys with TalkBack. With Live open and a chord held, switch Android's dark mode (quick settings), the system language or the font size, which do recreate arc: the debug log should show no new read of the device and the notes should fade out, not cut off (arc's own Theme setting doesn't recreate it, so it doesn't test this).

If anything fails, export the SysEx log from the debug screen (**Share log** or **Save log**) and attach it to an issue together with the error text.
